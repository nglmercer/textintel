# Speech lexical resources

`numbers.json` is embedded, versioned lexical data for the speech number
verbalizer. It is not a model and has no inference/runtime dependency.

## Sources and preparation

- The initial en/es/zh/hi/ar/fr/pt/ru/id/de/ja/tr/ta/vi/ko standalone 0–99
  words and contextual hundred/thousand prefixes were prepared with ICU
  **78.3**, whose RBNF data derives from **Unicode CLDR 48.0** (confirmed
  using `ulocdata_getCLDRVersion`). Primary rule
  files: <https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr/tree/release-48/common/rbnf>.
  The Unicode License V3 notice is included in `LICENSE-UNICODE.txt`.
- Marathi 0–99 labels are adapted from the BSD-licensed
  [libnumbertext mr.sor](https://github.com/Numbertext/libnumbertext/blob/73d0bb4bb8106a0f2f6d7b68ec1727b249e9299f/data/mr.sor),
  attributed there to Ankur Joshi. The upstream BSD notice is retained in
  `LICENSE-NUMBERTEXT.txt`. Word facts are not trained parameters.
- Bengali, Urdu, Punjabi (Gurmukhi), and Telugu are curated lexical tables;
  no external formatter is used at runtime. They need broader native-speaker
  review before a production pronunciation claim.
- The initial CLDR output was deliberately reviewed rather than trusted as a
  speech oracle: discretionary German soft hyphens were removed; Chinese
  zero is 零; Japanese uses explicit kana readings; Arabic uses standalone
  nominative dual forms; Tamil linking stems and hundred/thousand compounds
  are explicit. Region/case/inflection variants are not silently claimed.

Preparation uses ICU's `RuleBasedNumberFormat(URBNF_SPELLOUT, Locale(code))`
for integers 0–99, and 100n/100n+1/100n+10/1000n/1000n+1 for n=1..9.
Those outputs initialize `small`, exact hundreds/thousands, and nonzero
prefixes. The corrections above and the curated locales are then applied.
The checked-in JSON is the authoritative input; builds do not fetch or
regenerate it. Tests validate resource shape and independently specified
spoken gold cases, not merely values generated by this preparation process.

## Explicit support

- All 20 locales support digit reading and exact decimal fractions, preserving
  signs and zeroes, with at most 128 digits per integer/fraction component.
- hi/bn/ur/pa/mr/ta/te standalone cardinals cover 0..999,999,999 with
  lakh/crore grouping.
- ar/id/tr/vi cover 0..999,999,999,999.
- en/es/fr/de/pt/ru/zh/ja/ko cover 0..999,999,999,999,999, preserving the
  corresponding Western or four-digit large-scale conventions.
- es/pt/ru feminine integer forms cover their standalone cardinal ranges,
  retaining masculine million/billion scale nouns. They do not replace
  arbitrary grammatical-case or counter selection.
- Larger, malformed, or leading-zero cardinal inputs return unsupported;
  leading-zero identifiers can explicitly request digit reading.

Representative choices are general Spanish, US-style English without
optional "and", French as used in France, Brazilian Portuguese large scales,
Simplified Chinese, Punjabi Gurmukhi, and Sino-Korean. Locale-base support
must not be mistaken for every regional pronunciation, dialect, grammatical
case, currency agreement, or an independent native-speaker accuracy audit.

See the Unicode [RBNF scope and limitations](https://unicode.org/reports/tr35/tr35-numbers.html#Rule-Based_Number_Formatting):
number spellout data alone does not supply the grammatical context required
for agreement in a sentence. The parser and verbalizer expose unsupported
cases instead of inventing that context.

## Composition audit

A development audit compared all integers 0..9,999 plus selected supported
scale boundaries and maximum values against ICU 78.3 / CLDR 48.0: 140,225
comparisons across en/es/zh/hi/ar/fr/pt/ru/id/de/tr/ta/vi/ko. Comparison removed
German soft hyphens, normalized Unicode NFC, and equated Chinese 〇 with 零.
This checks composition against the table source; it is not an independent
linguistic accuracy measurement. Generated reference rows are not shipped as
gold tests.

The audit found and fixed Spanish apocope before scale nouns, Korean 일 before
억, and Vietnamese lẻ after a million-scale group. Representative regressions
are retained in `tests/speech_numbers.rs`. en/es/hi/fr/pt/ru/id/de/tr/vi/ko had
no remaining reference differences in the audited samples. Arabic matched
after the documented spelling and nominative-dual corrections; the raw ICU
comparison differs for 1,900 of its 10,000 samples.

Chinese has one unresolved style difference in the larger samples:
1,001,001,001 is rendered 十亿零一百万一千零一 here, while ICU emits
十亿一百万一千〇一. A fluent reviewer should decide the desired gap-zero
convention. Tamil differs from raw ICU in 9,966 of 10,000 samples because the
explicit compound/linking-stem corrections affect most compounds; raw ICU is
therefore not an accuracy oracle for that locale. Japanese kana readings and
the curated bn/ur/pa/mr/te tables require separate pronunciation and composition
review. Supported ranges describe implemented bounds, not native-speaker
certification of every value in those bounds.

## Shared date, currency, and unit dictionaries

`lexicons.json` is the central dictionary consumed by the speech renderer,
quantity grammar, and temporal grammar. It contains month names and localized
noun patterns across the same 20 base-language conventions as `numbers.json`.
The quantity vocabulary has 55 unit keys and 37 currency codes. Additional
unit patterns and currency names come from pinned Unicode CLDR JSON **48.0.0**:

- [Long unit patterns](https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr-json/tree/48.0.0/cldr-json/cldr-units-full/main).
- [Currency names](https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr-json/tree/48.0.0/cldr-json/cldr-numbers-full/main).

The Unicode License V3 notice is retained in `LICENSE-UNICODE.txt` and in
`lexicons.rs`. Only dictionary data is bundled; there is no network dependency
at build or runtime. Written aliases resolve once to canonical unit keys;
renderers reuse the same noun patterns and numeric grammar for quantities,
clock components, and explicit labelled measurements. Fullwidth aliases retain
original UTF-8 offsets. Compound quantities retain denominator semantics.

The dictionary's language coverage is distinct from correctness on every
possible noun agreement or source convention. The parser keeps ambiguous
currency symbols unresolved unless the caller supplies an ISO currency policy;
symbol-specific policies can distinguish `$`, `¥`, and `￥` in the same input.
Natural currency rendering includes supported minor-unit forms and named
scales without converting through floating point. Unknown denominations,
unsupported precision, or unavailable grammatical forms still require a
caller decision rather than an invented reading.

Extended Telugu scale rules use the primary
[CLDR release-49 Telugu RBNF](https://github.com/unicode-org/cldr/blob/release-49/common/rbnf/te.xml),
which adds coverage missing from release 48. Tamil and Arabic scale composition
reuse CLDR 48 vocabulary with explicit linking/dual/plural choices. The curated
basic ordinal vocabulary covers 1–10 for Arabic, Russian, Hindi, Bengali, Urdu,
Punjabi, Marathi, Tamil, and Telugu; this is a bounded closed vocabulary, not
unbounded ordinal grammar or native-speaker certification. Korean cardinal
zero is `영`, while digit-by-digit telephone zero remains `공`. Natural Russian
rational decimal readings cover six fractional digits, Arabic four, preserving
the written denominator precision; longer fractions retain digit readings.

Arabic currency tables explicitly implement standalone nominative one/dual
forms and 3–10 plural forms for selected currencies. The singular accusative
form is used with 11–99; unvocalized written alif is retained where required.
General number/noun agreement follows primary university grammar references:
[Al-Mustaqbal University number/noun notes](https://uomus.edu.iq/img/lectures21/MUCLecture_2025_6129126.pdf).
The currency noun forms are explicit lexical choices; case in an arbitrary
surrounding sentence still needs source context. For example, canonical MSA
`ثلاثة دولارات` differs from an invariant loanword form used in some external
fixtures. Such differences are preserved in the independent gold rather than
rewritten to inflate exact agreement.

Japanese/Korean large-cardinal bounds include 兆/조 (10^12). Japanese phonetic
allomorphs include `いっちょう`, `はっちょう`, and `じゅっちょう` at the
corresponding trillion quantities; the orthographic form retains `兆`.
Additional Telugu scale and basic ordinal rules: Copyright © 2026 Unicode,
Inc., CLDR 49 `te.xml`; Unicode License V3 applies.

Written-unit parsing indexes all bundled noun cases and plural patterns.
Additional Russian `килограммов` / `граммов` aliases coexist with CLDR's
zero-ending counting forms; they are supported genitive plural vocabulary,
not whole-input fixture replacements. See the primary
[Yerevan State University Russian grammar exercises](https://lib.ysu.am/open_books/416386.pdf).
Case-sensitive SI prefixes and bit/byte symbols retain their distinctions:
`MW` differs from unsupported `mW`, `MHz` from unsupported `mHz`, and `MB`
(megabyte) from `Mb` (megabit). Unknown prefix/case combinations are preserved
rather than assigned an incorrect magnitude. Unambiguous tolerance such as
fullwidth `ｋｇ` and uppercase `KG` remains supported.

Applications can supply exact, case-sensitive `unit_aliases` when their domain
uses additional written conventions. For example, a declared `f` →
`temperature-fahrenheit` policy enables both prefix and suffix forms; without
that caller policy an identifier such as `f9` remains an identifier. Custom
aliases operate on individual unit tokens and canonical unit keys, never on
complete input/output examples. The central dictionary supplies all 55 unit
keys and all 37 currency codes for every one of the 20 language conventions;
this verifies dictionary presence, not complete inflection or pronunciation
accuracy on all combinations.

Arabic spoken temperature patterns apply feminine degree agreement and
explicit singular/dual/plural unit wording. These are speech-oriented
adjustments to display-only CLDR wording; default MSA noun agreement can
differ from a reference that omits the degree noun entirely.

Measured mathematical expressions compose physical units with typed operands.
Natural English fraction measures use singular unit bases (`one half kilogram`,
`three fifths of a kilogram`); mixed quantities retain plural units. Formal
fraction fallbacks put the unit in the numerator before the division operator.
Mixed numbers are reduced with checked integer arithmetic, preserving the sign
of the complete quantity and returning unsupported on overflow. Scientific
measurements place the unit with the mantissa before the dimensionless power.
Native and caller-provided unit aliases share the same expression boundaries
as ordinary quantities. These mathematical templates are separate from natural
prose and remain subject to native-speaker review.

Vietnamese `length-centimeter` also recognizes the spelled unit tokens
`xăng-ti-mét` and `xăng ti mét`. The Ministry of Education and Training's
[national mathematics curriculum](https://moet.gov.vn/content/vanban/Lists/VBDT/Attachments/1559/2.%20Ch%C6%B0%C6%A1ng%20tr%C3%ACnh%20m%C3%B4n%20To%C3%A1n.pdf)
explicitly identifies `cm (xăng-ti-mét)`; the canonical CLDR display wording
remains `xentimét`. These are source-documented aliases of one typed unit,
not a general replacement of Vietnamese letter sequences. The reviewed
variant comparator records this lexical alternative separately from exact
output agreement.
